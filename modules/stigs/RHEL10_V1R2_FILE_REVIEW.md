# RHEL 10 V1R2 whole-file review

The RHEL 10 assets were reviewed on 2026-07-18 using the official DISA
`U_RHEL_10_STIG_V1R2_Manual-xccdf.xml` benchmark (Release 2, benchmark date
01 July 2026) and the `R10-STIG` RHEL 10.2 reference VM.

## Method

The review selected safe static destinations already represented below
`modules/stigs/files/` and one new dconf keyfile required by V-281286. Existing VM
files were retained in full. V1R2-controlled directives were then replaced or
appended with format-aware merging. Files absent because their package was not
installed used the existing complete reviewed module asset as a baseline;
files that are themselves STIG-created drop-ins contain the complete V1R2
fragment.

RHEL 10 content that differs normally uses a `.10` suffix and is generated by
`tools/generate_rhel10_v1r2_overlays.ps1` together with
`tools/merge_stig_exemplars.ps1`. Identical assets are consolidated after the
merge. Explicit common-policy exceptions are the shared
`security/pwquality.conf`, based on the former RHEL 8 asset, and the shared
emergency and rescue systemd units, based on the former RHEL 9 emergency unit
and existing unnumbered rescue unit. The RHEL 10 coredump configuration is also
the common policy for all three releases. The common profile timeout script
retains its shebang and the shared `TMOUT=600` control while discarding the
RHEL 10 variant's inaccurate `900` comment. Audit rules are extracted from the
XCCDF in benchmark order. The
unmatched parenthesis in the V-281158 64-bit `/etc/security/opasswd` path is
corrected to agree with its 32-bit rule and check text.

## Results

- 68 RHEL 10 STIG destinations are represented. Whole-file comparison found
  46 exact matches with existing assets. After also promoting the former RHEL
  8 password-quality policy and common emergency/rescue units for all
  releases, plus the RHEL 10 coredump and shared profile timeout policies, 17
  release-suffixed `.10` files remain.
- The Puppet module deliberately applies a common nine-file module deny policy
  to all three supported releases. The additional `atm`, `cramfs`,
  `firewire-core`, and `uvcvideo` files on RHEL 10 are common defense-in-depth
  policy and are not counted among the 68 V1R2-controlled destinations.
- `/etc/audit/rules.d/audit.rules` contains 75 event rules, failure mode
  `-f 2`, and terminal immutable mode `-e 2`.
- `/etc/ssh/sshd_config` retains the RHEL 10 vendor file and places the V1R2
  directives before the vendor `Include`, preserving OpenSSH's first-value
  precedence.
- `/etc/systemd/coredump.conf` starts from the RHEL 10 vendor file in `/usr`
  because no main file existed below `/etc`.
- RHEL 10 dconf settings include the V1R2 empty Ctrl-Alt-Del binding in the new
  `00-media-keys` asset and use the banner text directly from the XCCDF.

## Deliberately unparameterized controls

The following V1R2 requirements need site data and were not invented during
the static-file merge:

- the ISSO/SA recipient behind the root mail alias;
- the approved NTP server;
- the remote audit log collector and transport encryption details;
- SSSD certificate-map domains, rules, and DOD trust anchors;
- role-specific firewalld rate limits and allowed services;
- USBGuard device policy, fapolicyd rules, AIDE paths and notification target;
- NFS mounts/exports and repository definitions; and
- GRUB users, password hashes, mount layout, and other host-specific state.

The R10-STIG exemplar's existing generic `logcollector` forwarding entry is
preserved as part of its complete rsyslog file; deployments must parameterize
that endpoint before deployment.

## Validation

The RHEL 10 VM accepted the generated SSH configuration with `sshd -t`, and
effective output confirmed all 19 V1R2 SSH values. `rsyslogd -N1`, `visudo
-cf`, `bash -n`, `systemd-analyze verify`, and `dconf compile` also completed
successfully against isolated temporary copies. No VM configuration was
changed.

The `.10` and shared assets are selected by RHEL 10 cases, guards, and release
selectors in the existing functional manifests. The RHEL 8/9 package,
service-state, and permission resources remain guarded to those releases. This
makes the reviewed whole-file controls deployable without presenting the
module as complete RHEL 10 STIG coverage.
